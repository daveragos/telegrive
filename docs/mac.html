Install Telegrive on a Mac

Telegrive for Mac is one app for Apple silicon and Intel Macs. It isn't in the App Store, and Apple hasn't checked it, so the first time you open it your Mac asks you to confirm in System Settings. It takes a couple of minutes.

Before you start

  • A Mac with macOS 12 Monterey or newer. To check, open the Apple menu and choose About This Mac.

  • Telegram on your phone, to sign in by scanning a QR code. You can also sign in with your phone number.

Install and open it

  1. Download Telegrive for Mac. It's one file, ending in .dmg.

    Download Telegrive for Mac

    72 MB · macOS 12 or newer · Apple silicon and Intel

  2. Open the file and drag Telegrive onto Applications. Then eject the Telegrive disk from Finder's sidebar; you won't need it again.

    Quicker, with Terminal. Paste this into Terminal and press Return, and Telegrive opens straight away, with no need for the next two steps.

    xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Telegrive.app

  3. Open Telegrive from Applications. Your Mac says it can't check Telegrive for harmful software. Click Done, not Move to Trash.

  4. Allow it in System Settings. Open System Settings›Privacy & Security, scroll down to Security, and click Open Anyway beside the line about Telegrive. Enter your Mac's password, then click Open Anyway once more. Your Mac remembers this for this version.

  5. Let Telegrive use its key. Telegrive keeps the key to what it stores on your Mac in your keychain. When your Mac asks whether Telegrive may use it, enter your Mac's password and click Always Allow.

  6. Sign in with Telegram. Telegrive shows a QR code. On your phone, open Telegram, go to Settings›Devices›Link Desktop Device, and scan it. Your drive opens, the same one your phone has.

On a Mac, Telegrive does more. Drop files and folders on its window to upload them. Keep folders in sync, so what you save into them goes to your drive. Show your drive in Finder, to open and save into it like a disk. And Telegrive stays in the menu bar when you close its window, finishing what it's sending.

Updating Telegrive

New versions are listed on the Releases page. Download the new file, drag Telegrive onto Applications again and choose Replace. Your Mac asks you to confirm the new version in Privacy & Security, and asks about the keychain once more: click Always Allow. You stay signed in, and your drive stays as it is.

Want to check a download? Every release lists its files in SHA256SUMS.txt.

If something goes wrong

There's no Open Anyway button

It appears only after you've tried to open Telegrive, and for about an hour after. Open Telegrive from Applications again, click Done, and then look in Privacy & Security.

It says Telegrive is damaged

That's what macOS says when the download was changed on its way, or when it doesn't trust where it came from. Download it again from this page. If it still says so, open Terminal and run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Telegrive.app, which tells your Mac you chose to open it.

Why doesn't Apple check it?

Apple checks apps whose makers pay for a developer account. Telegrive is free and made by one person, and its code is on GitHub for anyone to read and build. Every release lists a checksum for each file, so you can make sure yours is the one published.

My Mac keeps asking about the keychain

Click Always Allow rather than Allow, and it stops asking until the next version. Each new version asks once, since your Mac sees it as a new app.

Is there a Windows or Linux version?

Not yet. They're on the way, with what the Mac version does.

Still stuck? Report a problem and say which macOS you have and what it showed.
